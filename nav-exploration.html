SeedSigner Docs / navigation explorations
Design exploration · 5 directions · August 2026

Five ways to find your way through 51 pages of hardware documentation.

Every direction below keeps the SeedSigner palette, the system type stack and the existing Docsify content model untouched. What changes is structure: where navigation lives, how much of it you can see at once, and what happens when the screen is 390 px wide and you are holding a soldering iron in the other hand.

Baseline

What the current navigation gets wrong

Measured directly against the running site at localhost:3000, not from memory. Every direction below fixes all six.

Scrolls away

The navbar is not fixed

The header sits position:absolute at the top of the document. Scroll into a long Reference page and the logo, the section links and any escape route are gone.

Buried

Search is inside the sidebar

On mobile the sidebar is closed by default, so reaching search costs a tap on a hidden control first. Search is the fastest path through a 51-page manual and it is the hardest thing to reach.

32 × 36 px

The menu toggle misses the target

The sidebar toggle measures 32 × 36 px — under the 44 × 44 minimum for a control sitting in the imprecise top edge of a phone screen.

Low value

The navbar spends its whole width on three outbound links

Website, GitHub and Community occupy the most valuable strip on the page and every one of them sends the reader away. All three already appear on the Home page and in Resources.

Duplicated

The logo lives in the sidebar

It costs ~90 px of vertical space at the top of the nav column, disappears entirely when the sidebar is closed, and leaves no persistent way home.

Fighting itself

~70 lines of CSS suppressing chevrons

index.html spends lines 684–756 cancelling expand affordances that two plugins keep adding back. That is a signal the collapse model is not the right fit for this tree.

Research

What the best documentation sites actually do

Structure and computed values read off the live DOM of each site, at 1280 px and at 375 px. The convergence between them is striking, and it is the shared baseline for all five directions.

platform.claude.com

Fixed 56 px header, sidebar docked beneath it

Header is position:fixed, full width, carrying product tabs. The sidebar is a 264 px fixed aside anchored at top:56px with its own scroll. Search is a button at the top of the sidebar reading “Search ⌘K”, not a live input. Active link is a white 10% pill at 8px radius — the fill carries state, weight stays 400.

vercel.com/docs

Sticky 64 px header, 300 px sticky rail

Header is sticky at z-index:75 with product tabs (Docs / Build / Learn). The sidebar is a 300px column, sticky at top:64px, scrolling independently. Below 880px it becomes a fixed panel dropping from under the header over a rgba(0,0,0,.5) scrim.

learn.chatgpt.com/docs

Two stacked bars, then a borderless 218 px rail

A fixed 64px header, a second 48px bar under it, then the sidebar docked at top:112px at only 218px wide with no right border — whitespace does the separating. Rows are 14px/400, 32px tall, 8px radius. Groups are flat and permanently open — no accordion anywhere.

Mobile, all three

The overlay wins

ChatGPT’s docs use a true full-screen panel (inset:0) sliding in from the right that merges primary nav and section nav into one list. Vercel drops a panel from under the header over a dimmed page. Nobody pushes content sideways.

Consensus

Search is never in the sidebar

All three surface search in or immediately below the fixed header, all three bind ⌘K, and all three keep it reachable at every scroll position.

Consensus

Section labels are quiet; the active row is loud

Group headings never shout — 12–14 px, muted or semibold, no uppercase in two of three. The entire visual budget goes to one row: the page you are on.

Applies to all five

The fixes every direction shares

01

Header pinned to the viewport

The navbar never scrolls away. Sidebar and content each scroll on their own beneath it — the structure you see in every frame below.

02

Logo in the navbar, links home

Moved out of the sidebar. Always visible, always the way back to the docs Home page.

03

Search out of the sidebar

Promoted into the navbar in every direction. Only the form differs: inline field, ⌘K button, icon, or hero field.

04

44 × 44 px minimum

Every icon control in the navbar — menu, search, close — is a 44 × 44 hit area, including the ones whose glyph is only 21 px.

05

Palette and type untouched

#f6851b, white surfaces, #4d4d4d body ink, the existing system stack. These are navigation explorations, not a restyle.

06

Reduced motion respected

Every slide, blur and push is wrapped in prefers-reduced-motion and degrades to an instant state change.

64 px. Logo, then five section tabs with a 2 px orange underline flush to the navbar’s own bottom border — so the active tab visually owns the sidebar beneath it. Home is the logo.

Why a sheet
You are already inside a section, so the menu only ever lists 3–27 items. A sheet keeps the page visible above it and sits in the thumb zone.
Motion
Tab switch is an instant swap — no crossfade, because a fade reads as loading. The sheet uses a 260 ms cubic-bezier(.32,.72,0,1) rise.
SignatureThe navbar tab strip. It converts a three-level tree into two levels and caps the sidebar at one screen, permanently.
What changedWebsite / GitHub / Community survive as 38 px icon-only buttons with title and aria-label — present, but no longer spending words. “Help & Resources” shortens to “Help” in the tab strip; the sidebar header restores the full name plus a one-line description of what the section is for.
Trade-offYou cannot see Reference’s contents while reading a Get Started page. Cross-section browsing costs one extra click, and in-page cross-links plus search have to carry that load. Also the weakest direction for “I don’t know what I’m looking for.”
Docsify notesNatively supported — Docsify resolves a per-folder _sidebar.md, so get-started/_sidebar.md, reference/_sidebar.md and so on give each tab its own tree with no plugin. The tab strip is a small hook.doneEach that marks the active tab from location.hash.

56 px. Menu, logo, then a live breadcrumb — Reference › Seeds › SeedQR backup — sitting in the navbar itself, so your position is stated even with the sidebar collapsed.

288 px on a #fbfbfc tint, true accordion: one section open at a time. L1 rows are 44 px and 15 px/600 — touch-sized even on desktop, because this direction assumes touch everywhere.
Structure
Get Started’s pages carry 01 02 03 indices. Reference’s do not. Numbering appears only where the content is genuinely a sequence — it is information, not decoration.
Mobile
Full-screen overlay rising 260 ms from the bottom. Pane one lists the six sections as 60 px rows; tapping one pushes the pane left to reveal its contents with a back row — iOS drill-down, so no list is ever long.
Orientation
A 40 px breadcrumb bar under the mobile navbar. You know where you are without opening the menu — the thing a hamburger normally costs you.
Motion
Two transforms, both cubic-bezier(.32,.72,0,1): the overlay rise and the horizontal pane push. Physical, directional, and reversible.
SignatureThe two-pane push overlay. Every screen shows one short list instead of one long tree, and the back row makes retreat as cheap as advance.
What changedAdds step indices to Get Started — new structural information, derived from the order already implied by _sidebar.md. Website / GitHub / Community move to a sticky sidebar footer, staying visible but ranked last. This is the only direction that keeps a menu icon on desktop, where it toggles a distraction-free reading width.
Trade-offAn accordion of one means jumping from Seeds to Settings closes Seeds behind you; and a full-screen overlay hides the page you were reading, which costs the context a side drawer would preserve. The breadcrumb is the compensation, not a cure.
Docsify notesThe most custom of the five. docsify-sidebar-collapse already provides accordion behaviour; the overlay needs a ~120-line plugin that clones .sidebar-nav into the two panes and drives is-drilled. The breadcrumb is derivable from the sidebar’s own active-ancestor chain — no front-matter needed.

Navbar
60 px, no bottom rule — just a 1 px shadow, so the header floats rather than divides. A labelled Menu button: an icon alone is a guess when it is the only route to the whole doc set.
Sidebar
None, by default. A 380 px panel slides over the content, elevated on 0 24px 60px -20px, flush to the viewport edge and full height beneath the navbar.
The blur
The page behind takes backdrop-filter: blur(6px) saturate(.85) under a 50% white veil. It stays legible enough to keep context but is decisively out of focus — the panel is unambiguously the foreground.
Replacement
A 200 px right-hand on-page TOC takes over within-page navigation, with an orange rule tracking the section you are reading. Reading width lands at 720 px, the widest of the five.
Escape hatch
Pin docks the panel permanently for readers who want a conventional sidebar; the content reflows and the choice persists in localStorage.
Mobile
The identical panel at 88% width with the identical blur. One implementation, one mental model, both breakpoints.
SignatureThe frosted panel over a defocused page. It is the only direction where the documentation reads like an article rather than an application.
What changedAdds an on-page table of contents — a navigation surface the site currently switches off (subMaxLevel: 0). Removes the permanent sidebar. Website / GitHub / Community move into the panel footer.
Trade-off — the real oneHiding navigation on desktop costs at-a-glance discoverability, which is the strongest single argument for a persistent sidebar and the reason none of the three reference sites do this. Pin, breadcrumb and TOC are mitigations, not equivalents. Choose this only if the analytics say readers arrive by search and stay on one page.
Docsify notesDocsify’s .sidebar is already position:fixed with a transform-based open state, so the panel is largely CSS — override width, add the shadow, and render the scrim. The TOC requires flipping subMaxLevel to 2 and rendering the anchors into a right rail. backdrop-filter needs a @supports fallback to a flat dim for older Firefox.

Where I would go

01 Ledger, with 05 Signal’s navbar search field.

Ledger is the only direction that both satisfies every constraint in the brief and removes code rather than adding it — the sidebar-collapse plugin, _navbar.md and ~70 lines of chevron-suppression CSS all go. For a 51-page manual read by people who mostly do not yet know what the device can do, showing the whole shape beats hiding it behind chevrons, and that is exactly the call learn.chatgpt.com and Vercel both made at larger scale.

Its two weaknesses are real and worth stating. Search is understated at 250 px — Signal’s treatment fixes that without disturbing anything else. And at 2,772 px it has the tallest sidebar of the five, which is the price of showing everything. Borrowing Signal’s denser row rhythm would cut that roughly in half without reintroducing a single chevron.

Both of Signal’s other ideas — per-section page counts, and the “Jump to” row — are additive and can land later, independently. So can Fieldnotes’ mobile breadcrumb bar. Ledger is the direction that leaves the most room for the others to be merged into it.
